## Highlight: MSc Dissertation (UCL)

**Robustness of Venture-Capital Exit Prediction to Structural Perturbations in Investor Co-Investment Networks: A Comparison of Tabular and Graph Neural Network Models**

*MSc Venture Capital and Private Equity with Financial Technology, UCL, September 2026. Supervisor: Li Zhang.*

I tested how reliable venture capital exit predictions are when the investor co-investment network changes. I used Refinitiv US VC deal data (2000–2024) and 11,791 startups from the 2010–2017 first-round cohort. I compared standard tabular models with a graph neural network (GNN).

**Key findings**
- **Network features did not improve accuracy.** Across all eleven model specifications, ROC-AUC ranged only from 0.64 to 0.66, and no difference between models was statistically significant.
- **Random changes to the network had little effect.** Removing highly connected investors had a much larger effect.
- **The GNN looked stable but was not.** Its ROC-AUC held steady, but its individual predictions were twice as likely to flip as the average tabular model's.
- **Main contribution:** network features add a different kind of instability. Removing a few central investors barely moves aggregate accuracy, but it reshuffles which startups the model ranks as likely to exit.

> **Skills**: Python, machine learning, graph neural networks, network analysis, VC data analysis

## University of Leeds: BSc Banking & Finance

### Dissertation: Private Equity's Impact on US Pharmaceutical Innovation

I studied how private equity investment affects innovation in US pharma, using firm-level data from 1985–2023 and t-tests, OLS, and random effects panel regressions. PE investment raised innovation output and R&D intensity without the negative leverage effects critics warn about.
